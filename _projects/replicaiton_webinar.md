## Replicating empirical studies in economics: an opportunity for students

I co-coordinated the free webinar series [Replicating empirical studies in economics](https://replication.uni-goettingen.de/wiki/index.php/Webinar_series:_Replicating_empirical_studies_in_economics_-_an_opportunity_for_students) along [Jan H. Höffler](https://www.graduateinstitute.ch/discover-institute/jan-h-hoffler) and [Diana Soeiro](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/authors/diana-soeiro/cv) during 2022. It was organized through the [Philosophy of Economics Working Group](https://ysi.ineteconomics.org/workinggroup/philosophy-of-economics) of the Institute for New Economic Thinking's *Young Scholars Initiative* (YSI). We brought together researchers, instructors, journal editors, and students to discuss how replications are designed, carried out, taught, and published across economics and neighboring social sciences.

The talks followed a flipped-classroom format: speakers shared pre-recorded presentations when possible, followed by live discussion sessions. Students were also invited to present work in progress, receive peer feedback, and develop their projects for publication.

## Sessions

### 1. Why replication? How is it done? Where can replication material be found?

**Gustavo A. Castillo Alvarez, Jan H. Höffler, and Diana Soeiro**<br>
September 8, 2022

[Pre-recorded presentation](https://vimeo.com/746369985) · [Live session](https://vimeo.com/channels/1810142/752803946) · [Audio](https://ysi-recordings.storage.googleapis.com/62e433cc1edc5f77bb1053a6/audio.m4a)

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speakers</summary>

The opening session presented the YSI Philosophy of Economics Working Group and introduced replication as a continuum: from rerunning an original study's data and code, to testing robustness or generalizability with new data and methods. It covered how to identify studies suitable for replication, share results, detect errors, contribute to scientific progress, and navigate resistance from authors and editors.

Gustavo A. Castillo Alvarez conceived the series through the YSI working group. Jan H. Höffler founded ReplicationWiki in 2013 and has researched and taught replication internationally. Diana Soeiro is a scientific writer and policy analyst whose work connects philosophy, public policy, governance, and sustainable development.

### 2. Ex post replication and ex ante documentation: two sides of a coin

**Richard Ball and Norm Medeiros, Project TIER (Haverford College)**<br>
September 15, 2022

[Teaching reproducibility using the TIER Protocol](https://vimeo.com/750000778) · [Using the TIER Protocol on OSF](https://vimeo.com/750000865) · [Live session](https://vimeo.com/channels/1810142/752827911) · [Audio](https://ysi-recordings.storage.googleapis.com/63109fac95dc3d02c3494faf/audio.m4a)

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speakers</summary>

This session distinguished ex post replication—independently reproducing results from a previous publication—from ex ante documentation: assembling and publicly sharing the data, code, and supporting information needed to reproduce a new study. It introduced [Project TIER](https://www.projecttier.org/) and showed why learning either practice helps students perform the other.

Richard Ball, Professor of Economics, and Norm Medeiros, Associate Librarian, are the co-founders and co-directors of Project TIER at Haverford College.

</details>

### 3. Replication in quantitative macroeconomics

**Robert Kirkby, Victoria University of Wellington**<br>
September 29, 2022

[Pre-recorded presentation](https://vimeo.com/752871416) · [Live Q&A](https://vimeo.com/755328940)

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speaker</summary>

Macroeconomics combines established public datasets with substantial, often study-specific computation. The session examined the field's still-developing replication practices: what successful and failed replications mean, how to choose and conduct a replication, when to contact original authors, how to make new work easier to reproduce, and where the field needs to improve.

Robert Kirkby is a macroeconomist at Victoria University of Wellington. His research develops theoretical and computational tools for macroeconomic policy analysis.

### 4. Computational reproductions may be messier than expected

**Nate Breznau, University of Bremen**<br>
October 13, 2022

[Pre-recorded presentation](https://vimeo.com/757911322) · [Live session](https://vimeo.com/760221837) · [Audio](https://ysi-recordings.storage.googleapis.com/6310a2b6bd0a0b02ba32dadc/audio.m4a) · [Crowdsourced replication paper](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/j7qta/)

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speaker</summary>

Computational reproduction means trying to regenerate a study's numerical results using its original data and statistical routines. Drawing on published work and a crowdsourced project involving 85 independent teams, this talk showed how missing code, computing environments, data versions, formatting, and decisions by both authors and replicators can prevent exact reproduction even when broad conclusions agree.

Nate Breznau researches social policy, public opinion, crowdsourced replication, and open science at the University of Bremen.

</details>

### 5. Student replication: “Effects of fiscal shocks in a globalized world”

**Franziska Strunz, Universität der Bundeswehr Munich**<br>
October 20, 2022

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speaker</summary>

This student project replicated Auerbach and Gorodnichenko's 2016 article, “[Effects of Fiscal Shocks in a Globalized World](https://doi.org/10.1057/imfer.2015.15).” It recovered the direction of the original effect but not the exact results, tested an extended sample period, and evaluated potential sources of the remaining differences.

Franziska Strunz was a PhD student and research assistant at Universität der Bundeswehr Munich, working in macroeconomics.

### 7. Reopening after COVID: a replication and extension of Chetty et al. (2020)

**Annais Gangolf, Devansh Goyal, and [Samuel Ross](https://www.samueleross.com/), Haverford College**<br>
November 10, 2022

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speakers</summary>

The authors replicated Section IV.A of Chetty et al. (2020), which analyzes how consumer spending, business reopenings, and employment responded to state reopening policies. Because the original replication materials were not public, they implemented the methods independently in Stata. Their estimates did not reproduce the originals exactly, but supported broadly similar conclusions. The project also proposed extending the analysis to additional reopening policies.

Original study: Chetty et al., “[The Economic Impacts of COVID-19: Evidence from a New Public Database Built Using Private Sector Data](https://doi.org/10.3386/w27431),” NBER Working Paper 27431. The presenters were undergraduate students at Haverford College.

</details>

### 8. Replication in political science

**Carolina Curvale and Gustavo Pérez-Arrobo, FLACSO Ecuador**<br>
November 17, 2022

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speakers</summary>

This presentation analyzed open-science practices and publishing requirements in leading political science journals, including preregistration, and surveyed their implementation in Latin America. It addressed both the promise of reproducibility for theory-building and the epistemological and methodological debates around making these practices disciplinary standards.

Related article: “[Documentation Requirements in Political Science Journals: Moving Towards Open Access Practices](https://doi.org/10.4067/S0718-090X2021005000108).” Carolina Curvale is a political scientist specializing in institutions and political economy in Latin America. Gustavo Pérez-Arrobo researches the political economy of development, research methods, and Latin American politics.

### 9. In what direction? Replication and extension of Oskooii (2020)

**Andrej Cvetić, Trinity College Dublin**<br>
November 17, 2022

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speaker</summary>

This project reproduced Oskooii's analysis of perceived discrimination and minority voting behavior, then extended it with European Social Survey data for the United Kingdom and Western Europe. The extension found that group discrimination lowered the propensity to vote, in contrast with prominent findings in the literature.

Andrej Cvetić was a PhD student and research assistant in political science at Trinity College Dublin, studying political psychology and immigrant integration in Western Europe.

### 11. Replication in behavioural economics

[Pre-recorded presentation](https://vimeo.com/776775790) · [Live Q&A](https://vimeo.com/803017781)

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speaker</summary>

The session reviewed large replication projects in behavioral economics and psychology, including why larger samples are often needed when original effect sizes are exaggerated. It also covered prediction markets and forecasting surveys for replication outcomes, along with reforms such as Registered Reports that can reduce publication bias and improve reliability.

Anna Dreber is the Johan Björkman Professor of Economics at the Stockholm School of Economics. Her research focuses on meta-science, behavioral economics, replication, and forecasting research outcomes.

### 13. Student replication: women’s participation in peace processes

**Lenka Olejníková, University of New South Wales**<br>
March 16, 2023

[Pre-recorded presentation](https://vimeo.com/804341171) · [Working paper and replication files](https://osf.io/ykna8/)

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract and speaker</summary>

This study examined the reproducibility of Krause, Krause, and Bränfors (2018), which found that women signing peace agreements significantly improves the durability of peace. The principal findings were reproduced despite minor inconsistencies, while additional analysis identified low statistical power and underspecified theoretical and empirical estimands as important limitations.

Lenka Olejníková completed a PhD at UNSW on the limitations of statistical modeling in research on gender and later worked at the intersection of child sexual abuse and domestic violence.

## Student replication projects

Students from economics and neighboring social sciences were invited to submit a short proposal describing:

- the study they wanted to replicate and why;
- whether they would use the original data and code or introduce new data or methods;
- what replication material was available;
- whether related replications already existed and how their project would add to them.

Coursework, thesis projects, individual work, and team projects were all welcome. Participants received feedback on their plans, an introduction to mutual peer review, and support in developing submissions for academic journals. The series also contributed to plans for a special issue of the *[International Journal of Pluralism and Economics Education](https://www.inderscience.com/jhome.php?jcode=ijpee)*.
